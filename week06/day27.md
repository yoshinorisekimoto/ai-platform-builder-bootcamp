# Day 27 — Stakeholder Alignment and Executive Commitment

## Objective
Practice aligning Product, Sales, Engineering, and partners while communicating uncertainty clearly to executives.

## Scenario
A strategic partner requested an onboarding improvement tied to a $4M expansion.

Key considerations:
- Partner onboarding completion: 62%
- Expected improvement: around 80%
- Global standard approach: 9 weeks
- Engineering confidence initially: 70%
- After a key platform dependency was resolved: 90%
- A critical regression issue could still delay launch by 1–2 weeks

## Recommendation
Do not overcomplicate the solution before understanding the real constraint.

If Product and the partner are already aligned, identify the remaining unresolved issue and ask the relevant stakeholder directly.

For commercial timing:

**“Does the nine-week timeline create any risk to closing the $4M expansion this quarter?”**

For the partner, provide as much certainty as the evidence supports, but do not create false certainty.

At 90% confidence, commit to the target launch date while clearly explaining the remaining regression-test risk.

## Executive Communication
A useful principle is:

**Give the partner confidence, but do not create false certainty.**

Senior communication should clearly distinguish:

**Target → Commitment → Guarantee**

These are not the same.

## Point of View
**I believe we should make decisions based on the real unresolved constraint, not create additional complexity before it is necessary.**

And when communicating externally:

**We should give partners as much certainty as we reasonably can, without promising what Engineering cannot yet support.**

## Human Review / Ownership
- Engineering owns technical confidence and dependency validation.
- Sales confirms the real commercial dependency.
- Platform Partnerships translates technical confidence into a clear partner commitment.
- The final external message should reflect both evidence and business impact.

## Key Learning
Senior presence is not about providing more solutions.

It is about identifying the one unresolved issue, asking the right question, and clearly stating what we can commit to and what we cannot.

## Plain-English Summary
First understand what everyone already agrees on.

Then find the one thing that is still unclear.

Ask the right person, use the evidence, and only promise what the team can realistically deliver.
