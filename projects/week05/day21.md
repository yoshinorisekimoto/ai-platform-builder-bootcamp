# Day 21: AI-Assisted Prioritization Under Commercial Pressure

## Objective

Use AI to structure competing business and technical priorities, then make a clear human recommendation with ownership.

---

## Scenario

A platform team can prioritize only one major API investment.

Three competing priorities exist:

- Protect 22% of existing revenue
- Pursue a $4M new revenue opportunity
- Maintain platform reliability and scalability

AI can help organize the evidence, compare options, and surface trade-offs.

The final decision remains with the human owner.

---

## Decision Framework

For each decision, I used the same structure:

1. My recommendation
2. The key reason
3. The trade-off
4. What I will personally drive
5. The business impact

This helped separate analysis from decision-making.

---

## Example 1: Protect Existing Revenue

### Recommendation

Prioritize the Bulk API improvement.

### Reason

22% of current revenue was at risk, and the company was prioritizing retention and profitability.

### Trade-off

New growth opportunities would be delayed.

### Ownership

I would personally work with Sales and the affected partners to protect renewals and track the new opportunities in parallel.

---

## Example 2: Change the Decision When Evidence Changes

New evidence showed that two customers were close to contract, representing $4M in annual revenue, and the Analytics API was a mandatory requirement.

### Recommendation

Change the priority to the Analytics API.

### Key Learning

A strong decision-maker should not defend an old recommendation when the evidence changes.

> The evidence changed, so my recommendation changed.

---

## Example 3: Balance Revenue and Platform Risk

Engineering could release the Analytics API in:

- 60 days with higher performance risk
- 90 days with full scalability testing
- 75 days with testing up to 500 requests per second

### Recommendation

Use a 75-day controlled rollout for the two priority customers.

### Reason

This preserved a realistic path to the $4M opportunity while reducing platform-wide risk.

### Trade-off

The broader release would be delayed.

### Ownership

I would personally align Sales, Engineering, and the two customers on:

- rollout scope
- testing criteria
- reliability thresholds
- expansion conditions

---

## Executive Communication Pattern

A useful structure is:

> My recommendation is...  
> The key reason is...  
> The trade-off is...  
> I will personally drive...  
> The business impact is...

This structure makes judgment, ownership, and impact visible.

---

## Key Learning

The biggest challenge was not technical knowledge or business judgment.

It was communicating a decision clearly enough that others could quickly understand:

- what I recommend
- why
- what I am willing to trade off
- what I will personally drive
- what business outcome I expect

AI can support the analysis.

The human must make the decision and own the outcome.

---

## Plain-English Summary

Good decisions are not enough.

A senior leader must make the recommendation clear, explain the trade-off, take ownership, and connect the decision to business impact.
